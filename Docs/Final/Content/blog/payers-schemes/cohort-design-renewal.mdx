# Designing Risk-Targeted Cohorts: A Payer's Guide to Renewal Leverage

## The Renewal Problem

Renewals are conversations about past performance and future risk. Your incumbent's talking points are usually:

- Claims trends (stable, slightly up)
- Pharmacy costs (managed)
- Utilization (within expected ranges)

Competitors offer the same story. So renewals become RFP beauty contests based on price.

What's missing? **Engagement as a risk lever.**

## Cohort-Driven Engagement

Zimasa's approach flips this. Instead of broad-brush member engagement, run **targeted cohorts** for specific risk groups:

### Example: Diabetes Cohort (Employer, 500 members)
- **Target**: Members with diabetes diagnosis + low preventive care engagement
- **Duration**: 90 days
- **Tracks**: MOVE (fitness facilities), CARE (dietitian + screenings), LEARN (diabetes education)
- **Incentive**: Partner gym discounts, lab screening subsidies
- **Measurement**: Active Days, screening completion, specialist visit completion

**Expected outcomes:**
- 50–70% activation (members with 8+ Active Days)
- 40% of cohort schedule preventive screening
- 25% engage with dietitian (new to program)

### Example: Renewal Conversation

"In Q2, we ran a 90-day engagement cohort targeting our 500 unscreened diabetic members. 65% participated in at least one health action. 40% completed preventive screening they hadn't scheduled in prior 2 years. This screening identified 12 members with hypertension requiring treatment — preventing future acute events. Cost of program: $X. Estimated preventive value: $Y."

This is concrete. Measurable. And a differentiation point vs. competitors.

## How to Design Your Cohort

1. **Identify the risk group** — High utilizers? Low preventive care? Specific condition?
2. **Choose the 90-day tracks** — MOVE, CARE, LEARN. Skip NOURISH if it's not relevant.
3. **Set the incentive mix** — Partner discounts, points, subsidies, or HSA contributions
4. **Define success metrics** — Active Days target, screening completion, specialist visits
5. **Plan the renewal story** — How will you present this data? What's the risk impact?

## Beyond the Numbers

Cohort design also builds member relationships. Members in organized programmes feel cared for. They're more likely to stay with your plan (retention) and recommend it (word-of-mouth).
