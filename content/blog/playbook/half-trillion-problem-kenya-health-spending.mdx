Your quarterly claims report has landed. Medical claims are up again. The wellness benefit priced into the scheme is barely used. And the members behind your largest claims are people you never heard from until they were admitted.

You're not alone.

Across Kenya, the same story is playing out in insurance company offices, HR departments and boardrooms. Kenya spends over **half a trillion shillings** every year on healthcare. Yet most of that money goes toward **treating sickness, not preventing it.** We've built a health system that's world-class at documenting illness, but not at keeping people healthy. It's great at paperwork, weak at engagement. That single design flaw turns small, solvable problems into big, expensive claims. And it's bleeding everyone dry.

---

## The Numbers Don't Lie

World Bank data put Kenya's current health expenditure at 4.3% of GDP in 2022. Against 2022 GDP of KSh 13.49 trillion, that is over KSh 580 billion a year (World Bank, World Development Indicators). About 24% of that spending — roughly **KSh 140 billion** — was paid out of pocket by households (World Bank, World Development Indicators, 2022), exposing families to financial risk even when they have some insurance coverage.

Only about **one in four** Kenyans has any form of health insurance: 26% of women and 27% of men aged 15–49 (KNBS and ICF, Kenya Demographic and Health Survey 2022). For everyone else, care is paid for at the point of need — and is often put off until it can't be.

Insurers feel the strain too. Underwriting losses across Kenya's general insurance business, which includes medical cover, rose from **KSh 3.72 billion** in Q4 2022 to **KSh 4.96 billion** in Q4 2023 (Insurance Regulatory Authority data, as reported by Korir and Vizi, 2025).

This isn't sustainable. This is a system under pressure.

## The Payer's Nightmare

If you're running a health insurance business, you're watching your bottom line like a hawk.

Claims come in faster than premiums. Fraud remains a persistent concern. And you have few tools to reach members before they need care.

Here's the brutal reality: by the time you see a claim, it's too late. Someone is already sick. Someone is already in the hospital. The cheap, simple choices — a screening, a follow-up visit, a prescription refilled on time — were missed years earlier.

You're processing claims for diabetes complications in 45-year-olds who were pre-diabetic at 40, but nobody prompted the next step. You're paying for heart attacks in members whose hypertension went unmanaged for a decade. You're covering stroke rehabilitation for members who never kept up with their medication.

> In the United States, the top 1% of people ranked by health spending account for about 22% of all health care expenditure, and the top 5% account for half of it (AHRQ, MEPS Statistical Brief #521, 2016 data).

Health spending is concentrated. Is yours? Many payers can see a similar pattern in their own claims book — and few have a structured way to engage those members earlier.

Your current systems are built to process sickness, not prevent it.

---

## The Employer's Dilemma

If you're an HR or benefits manager, you're caught in an impossible bind. Healthcare costs keep rising. You've done the best you can within the budget you have, but employees keep exhausting their cover.

You've thrown in a few token wellness talks and check-ups. Team-building exercises now count as "wellness." Large corporates with generous budgets have gone further: comprehensive health benefits, wellness programmes, gym subsidies, mental health resources, preventive screenings.

On paper, your benefits package looks great. But here's what's actually happening:

- Employees run out of cover before the year ends, and pay out of pocket.
- Wellness benefits often go unused.
- Employees don't understand what benefits they have until they need them.
- Many companies are turning to self-funded schemes because traditional insurance has become too expensive.

And the worst part? You have no way to see whether any of it is being used. Are employees taking up screenings? Are they following through? Is your multi-million-shilling wellness investment reaching anyone at all? You're flying blind — spending millions, and hoping for the best.

---

## The Member's Frustration

If you're an employee with health insurance, your experience probably looks like this: you were informed by HR that you've been onboarded onto a medical scheme. You get an insurance card you throw in a drawer. You vaguely know you have some coverage, but you're not sure what's included until you're sick and need it.

When you do need care:
- Hospital visits involve long queues, guesswork, and back-and-forth approvals.
- Pre-authorisation is a mysterious black box that sometimes says yes, sometimes no.
- Your benefits run out mid-year, and you're suddenly paying out of pocket for a procedure you thought was covered.

You know that wellness and prevention could save you from this frustration, but where do you start? If you're lucky, you have access to screenings or gym subsidies. But you've never actually used them. You're busy. You don't know how to sign up. Nobody's ever explained why you should care.

---

## What Needs to Change

This isn't about bigger limits or more product variants. It's about activation.

The future of health benefits must focus on:
- **Activating members before a crisis**
- **Pre-authorising prevention**
- **Sustained engagement through nudges and micro-incentives**
- **Measuring what matters** — activation, Active Days, screening completion and follow-through at 30, 60 and 90 days — not just wellness talks, team-building, and sporadic check-ups.

What Kenya needs is a **Health Engagement OS** — a system that unites payers, providers, employers and members to act *before* illness strikes, and that makes engagement measurable.

Not more paperwork. Not more claims. More health.
