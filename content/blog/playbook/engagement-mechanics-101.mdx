# Engagement Mechanics 101: Why Community Beats Compliance

## The Compliance Trap

Most corporate wellness programs operate on a simple assumption: if you mandate health participation, people will participate. Sign up for the program. Attend the screening. Complete the assessment.

The reality? 80% of employees never engage beyond the initial onboarding email.

Why? Because compliance-driven programs feel like obligations, not opportunities. They're designed around organizational needs (risk reduction, insurance credits), not member needs (motivation, community, progress).

## The Community Lever

Zimasa's engagement model flips this. We use four levers:

1. **Peer Accountability** — Squads of 3–5 people create social pressure to show up. Not punitive. Just present.
2. **Progress Visibility** — Leaderboards and challenges let members see their progress relative to peers. Competition drives engagement.
3. **Verified Outcomes** — Members earn points for verified actions (steps logged, classes attended, screenings completed). No guessing.
4. **Incentives That Matter** — Rewards tied to partner discounts, cash-back, or charity donations. Real value.

## Active Days Measure What Matters

We don't measure "enrollment." We measure **Active Days** — days where a member completed at least one verified health action.

In 90-day pilots, we see:
- 50–70% of eligible members achieve at least one Active Day
- Engaged members average 8–12 Active Days per month
- Track mix stabilizes at 40% MOVE, 35% CARE, 20% NOURISH, 5% LEARN

These numbers matter because they predict retention, health outcomes, and ROI.

## Start with Squads

If you're launching an engagement program, start with squads. Pick 5–10 teams of employees who already know each other (same department, office, or affinity group). Give them one challenge: log steps together for 4 weeks.

You'll see:
- Peer accountability working
- Natural competition emerging
- Early adopters recruiting peers
- Real Active Days data

Then expand. Add challenges. Introduce CARE (provider network). Layer in Rewards.
